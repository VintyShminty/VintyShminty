## Morning: security audit of the website, the app and the server

### What was done

- A full security read of the three parts: the server both the website and the app talk to, the website, and the phone app. Three reviewers read in parallel, one per part, and the two findings were checked by hand against the code afterwards.
- The server came back clean. Every page that reads or changes a person's listings, orders, offers, chats, addresses or money checks that the person asking is allowed to. Admin pages check the role in the database on every request. Photo uploads can only land in the uploader's own folder. Prices at checkout come from the database, never from the phone. Live chat rooms can only be joined by the two people in the conversation. The scheduled jobs need the secret. No keys or secrets are hidden in the app or the website.
- Two real issues were found, both medium. Nothing was changed. The handoff for fixing them is `docs/plans/2026-10-16-security-fixes-handoff.md`.

### Findings

1. Website: the "send me back after login" link can point to another site. When a page needs login, the website remembers where to send the person afterwards, and a check in `apps/web/lib/login-links.ts` is meant to allow only places on our own site. A hidden tab character in the address slips past it: browsers drop the tab, and the address then reads as another website. A scammer could send a logged-in person a link that looks like our login page and have them land on a fake "session expired" page.
2. App: the Face ID lock does not cover the sell form. The lock screen is a sheet drawn over the app from the root layout. The sell form (and onboarding) open as a separate native full-screen layer that iOS puts on top of everything, so it sits above the lock. Someone holding the unlocked phone could open the sell form from a link (`vinty://sell-form`) with the lock underneath and publish listings in the owner's name. Messages, orders and other private pages stay covered. Pop-up sheets that were open when the app locked may have the same gap, to be checked on the phone.

Below the bar, for awareness only: deleting an account and clearing someone's login codes use a loose "contains this email" match, which in theory could clear another person's pending code. The AI search and help helpers can be called without logging in, which is a cost question, not a security hole.

### Decisions

- Eden asked for the findings to be logged and handed to a separate session to fix. No fix was made here.

## Late afternoon: App Store seller name and payments research

Branch: `feat/home-top-wordmark` (no code changes, research and advice only)

### What was done
- Explained how the App Store seller name works. A personal Apple developer account always shows Eden's own name. A company name needs a company ("Organization") account, which needs a real limited company (Ltd) and a D-U-N-S number (a free company ID). An osek patur does not count, Apple treats one-person businesses as individuals.
- Explained that Kept and Yadii can both stay on Eden's personal account for now. When a Ltd exists, only Yadii can be moved to the company account with Apple's app transfer. Users keep the app, its ratings and its updates. The app must have had at least one public release first, TestFlight alone cannot be moved.
- Researched Israeli payment companies that can split a payment between buyer, seller and us, hold the money until delivery, and take Apple Pay. Compared PayMe, Rapyd, Cardcom, Tranzila, PayPlus, Grow, Hyp and Stripe.
- Described the "money on hold" flow: the buyer pays, the payment company holds the money, the seller ships, the buyer confirms (or the app confirms after a few quiet days), then our server tells the payment company to release it to the seller, minus our commission. A problem means a refund instead.

### Decisions
- Eden: launch under his personal Apple account for now. Apple pays nothing for Yadii (physical goods, no in-app purchases), payments go through an Israeli payment company, and the money goes to whoever owns the business.
- No payment company chosen yet. Payments stay in "Not added yet" in `CLAUDE.md` and the roadmap.

### Problems and fixes
- First recommendation was PayMe, but its marketplace product expects sellers to be businesses. Eden pointed out Yadii's sellers are private people. That moves Rapyd ahead (it can give private people wallets and pay to Israeli bank accounts), with PayMe as a maybe. Lesson: start from "who gets paid" before comparing providers.

### Open questions
- Which payment company? Default: send Rapyd and PayMe the four questions below, pick the first that says yes to all.
  1. Can you pay out to private people with no business registration, to a bank account or Bit?
  2. What do you need from each private seller, and how fast are they approved?
  3. Can you hold the buyer's payment until we confirm delivery, and for how many days at most?
  4. Does all of this work with Apple Pay?
- Legal: Israel's 2023 payment services law needs a licence for a marketplace that moves money between buyer and seller. Default: let the licensed payment company hold the money (never our own bank account) and get a short lawyer's opinion before launch, including whether the small-volume exemption applies.
- Tax: the business is registered to a partner while the Apple account is in Eden's name. Default: check with an accountant before launch (osek patur is one person only and has an income cap).

### Next steps
- Eden sends the four questions to Rapyd and PayMe.
- When one confirms, plan the real checkout on top of the existing order steps, and ask sellers for payout details only at their first sale.
- Before any future app transfer: prepare the Sign in with Apple handover on the server, or Apple sign-in users lose their accounts.
